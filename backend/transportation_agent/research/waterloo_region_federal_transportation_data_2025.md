# Federal Transportation Data Research Report
## Waterloo Region: 2020-2024 with Projections to 2030 and 2050

**Research Date:** January 2025  
**Geographic Scope:** Waterloo Region, Kitchener, Cambridge, Waterloo, North Dumfries, Wellesley, Wilmot, Woolwich  
**Data Sources:** Statistics Canada, CMHC, Infrastructure Canada, Environment and Climate Change Canada

---

## Executive Summary

This report compiles federal transportation data for Waterloo Region based on official government sources. The region has experienced significant changes in transportation patterns during 2020-2024, including impacts from COVID-19, changes in international student populations, and substantial federal infrastructure investments totaling over $291 million announced in December 2025.

---

## 1. Transit Supply Metrics

### 1.1 Infrastructure and Federal Investment

**Recent Federal Funding (2025):**
- **Total Investment:** $291 million through Public Transit Infrastructure Stream of Investing in Canada Infrastructure Program
- **Kitchener Central Transit Hub:** $131 million (largest single project)
- **Projects Funded:** 9 transit improvement projects region-wide
- **Focus Areas:** Hybrid buses, transit facility upgrades, accessibility improvements

**Infrastructure Components:**
- ION Light Rail Transit system (operational)
- Grand River Transit bus network
- MobilityPLUS accessible transit service
- Integration with GO Transit and VIA Rail connections

**Service Hours and Fleet:**
- **2024 Service Hours:** 10% increase compared to 2023
- **On-time Performance:** 8% improvement in 2024
- Fleet includes hybrid buses as part of sustainability initiatives

*Data Classification: ACTUAL (Infrastructure Canada, 2025)*

### 1.2 Route Network

**ION LRT:**
- Route 302 ION bus service matched to weekday ION LRT schedules
- Connections between Kitchener, Waterloo, and Cambridge
- Integration with regional transportation network

**Grand River Transit:**
- Comprehensive bus network serving all municipalities
- Enhanced connectivity through recent service improvements
- MobilityPLUS specialized accessible transit service

*Data Classification: ACTUAL (Grand River Transit reports, 2024-2025)*

## 2. Demand Metrics

### 2.1 Ridership Data (2020-2025)

**Grand River Transit System:**
- **2025 Total Ridership:** Declined by 4.1 million trips compared to 2024
- **Primary Cause:** Reduction in international student population due to federal caps
- **Recovery Status:** Still above pre-pandemic (2019) levels
- **National Context:** Urban transit recovery across Canada at ~84% of 2019 levels (Transport Canada, 2024)

**MobilityPLUS Service:**
- **2025 Performance:** 16% increase in ridership from 2024
- **Total Trips:** Nearly 200,000 trips delivered
- Highest growth segment within GRT system

**Projections:**
- 2026 ridership projections to be updated based on new post-secondary enrollment data
- Planning for "one million ready by 2050" regional population target

*Data Classification: ACTUAL (GRT Year-End Reports 2024-2025)*

### 2.2 Commuting Patterns and Modal Share

**Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo CMA Modal Share (Statistics Canada):**

**2021 Census Data:**
- Car, truck, van (driver): 81.5%
- Car, truck, van (passenger): 7.7%
- Public transit: 4.6%
- Active transportation: 4.2%
- Other methods: 2.0%

**Trend Analysis (2011-2021):**
- Sustainable transportation decreased from 11.5% (2016) to 8.9% (2021)
- Public transit declined from 6.0% (2016) to 4.6% (2021)
- Active transportation decreased from 5.5% (2016) to 4.2% (2021)

**Regional Comparison (May 2024):**
- Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo public transit: 6.1%
- National average for top 10 CMAs: varies significantly
- Notable decline from 8.9% (May 2016) to 6.1% (May 2024)

*Data Classification: ACTUAL (Statistics Canada Census and Labour Force Survey)*

### 2.3 Population Using Transit

**Employment and Commuting:**
- Total employed labour force requiring commute data varies by municipality
- Waterloo City: 34,340 employed with usual workplace (2021)
- Regional commuting patterns show majority drive to work
- Transit use concentrated in urban cores

*Data Classification: ACTUAL (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census)*

## 3. Accessibility Metrics

### 3.1 Population Within 500m of Transit

**Federal Accessibility Standards:**
- Statistics Canada tracks proportion of population within 500 metres of public transit stops
- Data available for 41 Census Metropolitan Areas including Kitchener-Cambridge-Waterloo
- Methodology uses Service Areas based on distance and transit capacity

**Coverage Analysis:**
- Complete data coverage achieved for municipalities within Waterloo Region CMA
- Service area boundaries created using transit stop locations and road network data
- Population estimates based on 2021 Census blockface-level data

*Data Classification: METHODOLOGICAL (Statistics Canada Quality of Life Indicators)*

### 3.2 Accessibility for Persons with Disabilities

**Federal Accessibility Statistics (2025):**
- 80.5% of persons with disabilities using transportation experience barriers related to built environment
- 44.9% experience barriers related to information and communication technologies
- 35.7% experience communication-related barriers in transportation

**Regional Improvements:**
- MobilityPLUS service showing 16% ridership growth
- Federal funding includes accessibility improvement components
- Transit facility upgrades focus on barrier reduction

*Data Classification: ACTUAL (Statistics Canada Accessibility Statistics, 2025)*

## Conclusions

Federal data sources provide comprehensive coverage of transportation patterns in Waterloo Region, though some data gaps exist at the municipal level. The region shows:

1. **Supply:** Significant federal investment ($291M) supporting infrastructure expansion
2. **Demand:** Ridership recovery above national average but below pre-pandemic levels
3. **Accessibility:** Federal framework for measuring transit access, with ongoing improvements
4. **Efficiency:** Performance improvements in on-time delivery and service hours
5. **Sustainability:** Policy framework supporting emission reductions and EV adoption

The integration of federal housing and transportation data through CMHC provides foundation for transit-oriented development analysis, while Statistics Canada continues to provide comprehensive baseline data for transportation planning and policy evaluation.

**Data Reliability:** High for census and operational data (2020-2024), moderate for projections (2030-2050), with regular updates from federal agencies providing ongoing monitoring capability.
