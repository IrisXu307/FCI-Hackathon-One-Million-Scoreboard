# Ontario Ministry Initiatives: Waterloo Region Placemaking (2020-2025)

## Overview

This document summarizes the specific initiatives, data sources, and engagement from Ontario provincial ministries related to Waterloo Region's placemaking, public spaces, walkability, accessibility, and community engagement.

---

## 1. MINISTRY OF TRANSPORTATION (MoT)

### Primary Responsibility
- Transportation infrastructure and sustainable mobility
- Active transportation (walking, cycling)
- Transit accessibility and integration
- Data collection on transportation behavior

### Key Data Sources

#### 1.1 Transportation Tomorrow Survey (November 2024 Release)

**Survey Scope:** 33 Ontario communities
**Data Collection:** Comprehensive transportation behavior assessment
**Release Pattern:** Annual releases
**Methodology:** Travel diary surveys, mode share analysis

**Waterloo Region Specific Findings:**

| City | Walking Rank | Weekly Trips | Status | Context |
|------|--------------|-------------|--------|---------|
| Waterloo | #2 in Ontario | 47,710 | Leading | Behind Toronto only |
| Kitchener | #25 | Lower | Lagging | Density-related |
| Cambridge | #27 | Lower | Lagging | Density-related |

**Key Insight:** Pedestrian friendliness correlates directly with urban density. Higher density = better walkability outcomes.

**Ministry Data URL:** Published via media releases; accessible through City of Waterloo data portals

#### 1.2 Transit Mode Share Data (2011-2024)

**2011 Baseline:** 5.3% of Waterloo Region trips used Grand River Transit
**Trend:** Growth expected post-ION implementation
**Context:** Shows baseline from pre-LRT infrastructure completion

**Collection:** Ongoing through Transportation Tomorrow Survey
**Application:** Infrastructure planning and investment justification

### Coordination with Waterloo Region

**Recent Initiatives:**
- Funding for Kitchener Central Transit Hub ($131M)
- Transit facility accessibility improvements
- Hybrid bus procurement support
- Active transportation infrastructure grants

**Engagement Point:** Ministry provides data; Region implements policies

**Contact:** Ontario Ministry of Transportation
- Website: https://www.ontario.ca/
- Transit Planning Resources: Available through regional partnerships

## 2. MINISTRY OF MUNICIPAL AFFAIRS & HOUSING (MMAH)

### Primary Responsibility
- Land use planning policy and framework
- Official Plans and zoning regulations
- Housing policy and supply
- Municipal governance and integration
- Placemaking policies embedded in planning

### Key Data Sources & Initiatives

#### 2.1 Regional Official Plan Review & Approval (2019-2020)

**Process Timeline:**
- October 2018 - January 2020: Comprehensive engagement
- Multiple Ministry meetings with Region and area municipalities
- Focus on Major Transit Station Areas (MTSAs)
- Housing and land use integration

**Ministry Engagement Activities:**
- 15-Jan-2019: Initial planning consultation
- 18-Jul-2019: Rural settlement and housing consultation
- Multiple MTSA technical workshops

**Key Focus Areas Discussed:**
- MTSA densification and design
- Transit-oriented development
- Housing affordability and supply
- Rural settlement policies
- Community design integration

**Document Reference:** PDL-CPL-22-24 (Engagement Summary)

#### 2.2 Bill 23 - More Homes Built Faster Act, 2022

**Implementation Date:** January 1, 2025
**Impact on Waterloo Region:** Significant planning authority restructuring

**Changes in Waterloo Region:**
- Regional Official Plan authority transferred to area municipalities
- Region retains coordination role
- Area municipalities now primary approval authorities
- Emphasis on housing acceleration

**Placemaking Implications:**
- Local official plans now primary planning documents
- City of Waterloo has independent planning authority
- City of Kitchener has independent planning authority
- City of Cambridge has independent planning authority
- Four townships have independent planning authority

**Current Status:** Transition completed January 2025

#### 2.3 MTSA (Major Transit Station Area) Planning

**Definition:** Areas within 800m of ION light rail stations
**Planning Framework:** Density targets, mixed-use requirements
**Placemaking Component:** Land use mix, public space integration

**Ministry Role:**
- Policy framework development
- Approval of MTSA plans
- Monitoring of densification progress
- Growth accommodation oversight

**Document:** Consolidated in Regional Official Plan; now in individual Official Plans

### 2.4 Public Spaces & Placemaking Policies

**MMAH Policy Framework (embedded in planning guidance):**
- Mixed-use development encouragement
- Heritage conservation requirements
- Community design principles
- Public realm integration
- Accessible community infrastructure
- Cultural amenities in new development

**Application in Waterloo Region:**
- Downtown revitalization initiatives
- Adaptive heritage reuse (The Tannery, Breithaupt Block, etc.)
- Arts and culture integration
- Public space design standards

## 4. MINISTRY OF HEALTH

### Primary Responsibility
- Population health and disease prevention
- Community health services
- Healthcare system coordination (through Ontario Health)
- Public health policy
- Community engagement in health

### Key Data Sources & Initiatives

#### 4.1 Ontario Health System Performance

**Framework:** Ontario Health manages regional health system integration

**Performance Indicators (2024-25):**
- Home care service wait times
- Nursing visit wait times
- Complex care coordination
- Community care accessibility

**Waterloo Region (Central East Health Region Data):**
- Complex needs support within 5 days: 87% (target 90%)
- Nursing visits within 5 days: 92% (target 95%)
- Home care wait time (50th percentile): 7 days (at target)
- Home care wait time (90th percentile): Variable by health region

**Source:** Ontario Health Annual Report 2024-25

#### 4.2 Public Health Ontario - Surveillance & Data

**Role:** Population health monitoring, data provision
**Waterloo Region Connection:** Provides surveillance data for regional public health

**Available Data:**
- Infectious disease surveillance
- Sexually transmitted infection trends
- Population health indicators (290+ indicators available)
- Seasonal illness reports

**2024-25 Seasonal Illness Report:** Published by Region of Waterloo Public Health
- RSV, COVID-19, Influenza vaccine distribution
- At-risk population identification and support
- Healthcare partner coordination

#### 4.3 Community Health Integration

**Ministry Role:** Policy framework for community-centered health

**Regional Implementation:**
- Community health assessment processes
- Population health planning
- Vulnerable population support
- Cross-sector collaboration (health, social services, housing)

**Immigration Health Support:**
- Settlement health services
- Language-accessible healthcare
- Culturally appropriate care
- Integration with settlement services

**Program:** Immigration Waterloo Region Community Action Plan (2020-2025)
- Health component: Access to healthcare for newcomers
- Community building through health initiatives
- Partner coordination between public health and settlement services

#### 4.4 Ontario Health Teams (OHTs)

**Framework:** Regional health system integration model

**Ministry Mandate:** Health system coordination and primary care integration

**Waterloo Region Implementation:**
- Local OHT coordinating regional providers
- $2.25M annual funding per team (provincial investment from 2024-25)
- Mental health and addictions integration
- Complex care coordination

## 11. KEY PROVINCIAL COORDINATION POINTS

### 11.1 Placemaking as Cross-Ministerial Priority

**Recognition:** Vision 1 Million Scorecard places placemaking/livability equal to:
- Housing supply (MMAH)
- Transportation (MoT)
- Healthcare (MoH)
- Employment (Economic Development)

**Implication:** Coordinated provincial approach vs. siloed ministry action

### 11.2 Bill 23 Impact on Provincial Coordination

**Pre-2025:** Ministry of Municipal Affairs coordinated regional planning
**Post-2025:** Area municipalities primary planners; Ministry provides framework

**Challenge for Coordination:** Ensuring cross-municipal consistency in placemaking standards

**Solution:** Provincial policy statements, growth plan requirements, funding incentives

### 11.3 Major Capital Projects as Placemaking Tools

**KCTH Example:**
- $131M transit investment
- Designed as community gathering node
- Active transportation integration
- Accessibility mandates
- Mixed-use development support

**Ministry Coordination:**
- Infrastructure (Federal-Provincial)
- Transportation
- Municipal Affairs
- Environment
- Finance (Treasury)

## 13. RECOMMENDATIONS FOR PROVINCIAL-REGIONAL ALIGNMENT

Based on the data reviewed, opportunities for strengthened coordination:

1. **Annual Data Harmonization Meeting**
   - Bring together: Ministries, Region, Area Municipalities, BestWR
   - Review: Vision 1M Scorecard, CTC Monitoring, provincial data
   - Alignment: Ensure policies match on ground implementation

2. **Placemaking Standards Development**
   - Establish: Provincial placemaking framework for post-Bill 23 area municipalities
   - Ensure: Consistency across Waterloo Region's 7 municipalities
   - Implementation: Technical support for local plans

3. **Data Integration Initiative**
   - Consolidate: Ministry data, regional data, city data into unified dashboard
   - Public Access: Accessible reporting on progress toward Vision 1M goals
   - Decision Support: Evidence-based planning and investment

4. **Community Engagement Excellence**
   - Coordinate: Health, municipal, business, community engagement efforts
   - Focus: Placemaking and livability (underinvested relative to housing/transport)
   - Mechanism: Community Data Program + deliberative engagement
