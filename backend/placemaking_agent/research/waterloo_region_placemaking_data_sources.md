# Waterloo Region Placemaking: Public Data Sources (2020-2025)

## Executive Summary

This document compiles publicly available data sources on placemaking, community engagement, economic development, and neighborhood vibrancy in Waterloo Region. Data covers research from universities, real estate boards, community foundations, municipal reports, and economic development agencies for the period 2020-2025.

### 3.1 Waterloo Region Community Foundation - Vital Signs® Reports

#### Overview
- **Program**: Community-driven data program (nationally coordinated by Community Foundations of Canada)
- **Approach**: Combines research data with resident lived experiences
- **Focus**: Social infrastructure, community well-being, and quality of life

#### 2025 Vital Signs® Report - Social Infrastructure
**Released**: September 10, 2025
**Focus Theme**: Social infrastructure – shared spaces, shared experiences, and shared resources enabling meaningful connections

**Key Findings**:
- Life satisfaction: Only 49% of residents report high life satisfaction
- Mental health declining more sharply in Waterloo Region than elsewhere in Canada
- Growth-to-satisfaction disconnect: Fastest-growing metropolitan area in Canada, but social infrastructure lagging
- **Critical Gap Identified**: Social infrastructure has not kept pace with rapid population growth

**Social Infrastructure Categories Measured**:
- Parks and community centres access
- Gyms and trails
- Libraries and cultural institutions

### 6.1 ION LRT (Light Rail Transit System)

#### Project Overview
- **Status**: Stage 1 operational (Kitchener-Waterloo corridor)
- **Investment**: $818 million
- **Stages**:
  - Stage 1: 22-mile corridor (12-mile LRT + 11-mile adapted Bus Rapid Transit (aBRT))
  - Coverage: Kitchener to Waterloo urban cores, aBRT to Cambridge
  - Recent: Stage 2 extension to Cambridge under consideration

---

#### Impact Monitoring - Central Transit Corridor (CTC) Reports
**Latest Monitoring Change in CTC Report**

**Year-over-Year Impacts Measured**:
- **Community Housing**: 3,077 units in CTC (330 more than 2011) = 12% increase
- **Transportation Patterns**: Ridership and modal shift data
- **Investment Trends**: Property and business development metrics
- **Environmental Impacts**: Emissions reduction
- **Dual Goals Assessment**: "Moving people" and "building community" progress tracking

**Key Finding**: Stage 1 ION exceeding goals on dual objectives of moving people and building community

---

#### Unique Features
- Connection to intercity transit (GO Train to Toronto, VIA Rail connections)
- CN freight train shared use (portion between Waterloo Public Square and Northfield stations)

## 10. RESEARCH NOTES & GAPS

### Available Data Strengths
1. **Strong university research** on urban planning, housing, transit, and placemaking
2. **Robust economic data** from multiple sources (EDC, BIAs, real estate boards)
3. **Community well-being data** from Vital Signs reports with both quantitative metrics and qualitative insights
4. **Regular demographic updates** via Statistics Canada
5. **Transit impact monitoring** with Central Transit Corridor reports
6. **Recent strategic planning** across municipalities (2023-2025 cycle)

### Data Gaps & Limitations
1. **Limited real-time foot traffic data** - No publicly available digital foot traffic metrics for neighborhoods
2. **Business district metrics** - Limited aggregate data on business occupancy, turnover, or revenue (mostly from BIAs)
3. **Event data completeness** - Events are scattered across multiple websites; no centralized regional event database
4. **Social cohesion metrics** - Limited quantitative data on neighborhood social networks (Vital Signs covers broadly but not neighborhood-level)
5. **Public space usage data** - Limited data on park/plaza/public space usage patterns
6. **Retail/commercial vibrancy** - Limited public commercial real estate data beyond residential MLS

### Data Access Notes
1. Most sources are freely accessible online
2. PDFs of reports available directly from source websites
3. Interactive data tools available for census data and some economic indicators
4. Academic research requires university access or open-access repositories

## 11. RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FUTURE DATA COLLECTION

1. **Establish centralized community event calendar** with attendance tracking
2. **Implement public space usage monitoring** (passive counting/sensors in parks and plazas)
3. **Develop neighborhood-level social infrastructure scorecard** (detailed geographic breakdown of Vital Signs)
4. **Create retail/commercial vibrancy dashboard** with vacancy rates, business turnover, operating hours data
5. **Partner with universities** to develop affordable, ongoing foot traffic monitoring systems
6. **Integrate real estate market data** with community engagement data for longitudinal analysis
7. **Establish baseline metrics** for placemaking success factors aligned with Vision 1 Million goals
