TOP TIER FINDINGS - WALK-ABILITY & PEDESTRIAN SUCCESS
================================================================================

✓ CITY OF WATERLOO: #2 IN ONTARIO FOR WALKABILITY (2024)
  Data Source: Ontario Ministry of Transportation, Transportation Tomorrow Survey
  Key Metrics:
    - Walking rank: #2 in Ontario (only behind Toronto)
    - Weekly walking trips: 47,710 (2024)
    - Walking trip percentage: ~18-20% of all trips (estimated)
  Release Date: November 2024
  Significance: Demonstrates success of compact urban design and density policies
  URL: https://kitchener.citynews.ca/2025/01/14/waterloo-likes-to-walk-while-cambridge-kitchener-lag-behind/

✗ KITCHENER & CAMBRIDGE: WALKABILITY GAPS
  Data Source: Same Ministry survey
  Key Metrics:
    - Kitchener rank: #25 out of 33 ON communities
    - Cambridge rank: #27 out of 33 ON communities
    - Lower weekly walking trips than Waterloo
  Issue: Density and sidewalk maintenance gaps
  Ministry Recommendation: Enhanced sidewalk infrastructure investment needed

TOP TIER FINDINGS - VISION 1 MILLION SCORECARD
================================================================================

✓ VISION 1 MILLION SCORECARD VERSION 1 RELEASED (JANUARY 2026)
  Organizations: BestWR + Future Cities Institute (University of Waterloo)
  
  Framework: 5 Interconnected Domains
    1. Housing Supply
    2. Transportation Infrastructure
    3. Healthcare Services
    4. Employment & Jobs
    5. PLACEMAKING & LIVABILITY (First-time major Ontario framework equal-weighting)
  
  Current Assessment (Version 1):
    Housing Supply: Advancing (positive)
    Employment: On Track (positive)
    Transportation: Needs Acceleration (challenge)
    Healthcare: Capacity Concerns (challenge)
    Placemaking & Livability: Framework Established, metrics refined
  
  Overall Regional Status: 
    "Currently, housing supply, transportation infrastructure, healthcare 
    services, employment opportunities, and social infrastructure is not 
    advancing at the pace needed overall to support [regional] expansion"
  
  Significance:
    - First major Ontario framework treating placemaking equal to housing/transit
    - Evidence-based decision making tool
    - Annual updates planned (Vision 1M v2 expected Q2 2026)
  
  URL: https://bestwr.org/

TOP TIER FINDINGS - PROVINCIAL PLANNING FRAMEWORK CHANGES
================================================================================

✓ BILL 23 IMPLEMENTATION - JANUARY 1, 2025
  Legislation: More Homes Built Faster Act, 2022
  Implementation Date: January 1, 2025
  
  Impact on Waterloo Region:
    BEFORE: Regional Official Plan as primary planning document
    AFTER: Area municipal Official Plans as primary documents
    
    Authorities Shifted to Area Municipalities:
    - City of Waterloo (independent planning authority)
    - City of Kitchener (independent planning authority)
    - City of Cambridge (independent planning authority)
    - Township of North Dumfries (independent planning authority)
    - Township of Woolwich (independent planning authority)
    - Township of Wellesley (independent planning authority)
    - Township of Wilmot (independent planning authority)
    
    Regional Role Changed:
    - Now coordination role vs. approval authority
    - Strategic planning and infrastructure coordination remains
  
  Placemaking Implication:
    Challenge: Maintaining cross-municipal placemaking consistency
    Opportunity: Local customization of placemaking priorities
    Provincial Role: Policy framework, funding incentives, performance monitoring

URL: https://www.regionofwaterloo.ca/en/doing-business/Regional_Official_Plan.aspx

REGIONAL READINESS ASSESSMENT (Vision 1 Million Context)
================================================================================

STRENGTH: Placemaking & Livability
  ✓ Waterloo #2 in Ontario for walkability
  ✓ $291M transit investment demonstrates commitment
  ✓ CTC monitoring shows investment concentration
  ✓ Heritage conservation policies active
  ✓ Arts and culture revitalization underway (Kitchener focus)

CHALLENGE: Infrastructure Pace vs. Growth Rate
  ✗ Overall region "not at pace to support growth"
  ✗ Transportation acceleration needed (per Vision 1M v1)
  ✗ Healthcare capacity concerns
  ✗ Housing supply advancing but affordability gap widening

ON TRACK: Employment
  ✓ Employment growth tracking population growth expectations
  ✓ Tech sector strength (University of Waterloo influence)
  ✓ Business cluster development

ADVANCING: Housing Supply
  ✓ Building permits at record levels ($1.8B in 2022)
  ✓ Intensification targets identified (60%)
  ✓ Affordability policy development ongoing

VERDICT: Waterloo Region positioned as growth leader with strong walkability foundation, 
but needs accelerated infrastructure investment and equity-focused policy to sustain 
1 million population vision by 2050.
