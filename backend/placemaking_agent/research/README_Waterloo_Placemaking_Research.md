## KEY FINDINGS SUMMARY

### Placemaking & Livability
✓ **Vision 1 Million Scorecard (Version 1, Jan 2026):** Framework established for measuring regional readiness
- Identifies placemaking/livability as equal priority to housing, transport, healthcare, employment
- Current status: Framework set; specific targets being refined

### Walkability (Ministry of Transportation)
✓ **City of Waterloo:** #2 in Ontario for walkability (2024)
- 47,710 walking trips per week
- Only behind Toronto in pedestrian-friendliness ranking
- Ministry data released November 2024

✗ **Kitchener & Cambridge:** Lag behind Waterloo
- Lower walking trip counts
- Correlation with density differences
- Sidewalk maintenance and winter clearing gaps identified

### Transit & Accessibility
✓ **$291 Million Provincial-Federal Investment (Dec 2025)**
- $131M for Kitchener Central Transit Hub (placemaking focus)
- Multi-modal transportation hub design
- Construction starting 2025; multi-year project

✓ **Accessibility Plans**
- City of Waterloo: 2025-2030 Multi-Year Accessibility Plan
- Region: 2020 Accessibility Status Report
- Grand River Transit accessibility improvements ongoing

### Central Transit Corridor (CTC) Monitoring
✓ **18 Key Indicators Tracking:**
- 2011-2024: Comprehensive monitoring of ION light rail impact
- Goals: "Moving People" and "Building Community"
- Building permit value: $1.8B in 2022 (record year, +6% YoY)
- Some 2024 indicators pending (cultural vibrancy, safety perception)

### Population & Growth
✓ **Kitchener Growth Projections 2024-2051:**
- Current population: 318,280 (2024)
- Projected population: 446,000 (2051)
- Growth: 127,720 residents (+40%)
- Housing need: 96,353 units (60% intensification target)

### Provincial Planning Changes
✓ **Bill 23 Implementation (Jan 1, 2025)**
- Regional Official Plan authority transferred to area municipalities
- Creates challenges for cross-municipal placemaking coordination
- Provincial guidance becoming primary tool for consistency

### Public Health & Community
✓ **Ministry of Health Integration:**
- Ontario Health performance data (wait times tracking)
- Community engagement through healthcare
- Immigration integration initiatives (2020-2025 plan active)
- Seasonal illness prevention and vaccination programs

## FINAL NOTES

### What This Research Shows
This research demonstrates that:
1. Ontario provincial government actively collects and publishes placemaking-related data
2. Multiple ministries coordinate (Transportation, Municipal Affairs, Health) on placemaking
3. Waterloo Region is positioned as a key growth region under Vision 1 Million framework
4. $291M transit investment shows provincial commitment to infrastructure-as-placemaking
5. Evidence-based metrics (Vision 1M Scorecard, CTC monitoring) guide provincial-regional collaboration
6. Significant gaps remain in equity analysis, cultural data, and livability measurement

### What This Research Doesn't Cover
- Detailed community resident input and satisfaction data
- Specific neighborhood-level analysis within Waterloo Region
- Indigenous community perspectives and data
- Environmental sustainability metrics beyond emissions
- Arts and culture detailed economic impact
- Detailed affordability/equity analysis
- Private sector placemaking initiatives (business improvement areas, etc.)
- Digital/technology placemaking dimensions

### Next Steps for Stakeholders
1. **Provincial Government:** Finalize placemaking metrics in Vision 1M Scorecard v2; strengthen cross-ministry coordination
2. **Regional Government:** Complete pending CTC indicators; develop equity-focused analysis
3. **Area Municipalities:** Develop consistent placemaking standards post-Bill 23; align with provincial framework
4. **Researchers:** Conduct deeper analysis of placemaking outcomes and equity impacts
5. **Community:** Use available data to advocate for continued placemaking investment
