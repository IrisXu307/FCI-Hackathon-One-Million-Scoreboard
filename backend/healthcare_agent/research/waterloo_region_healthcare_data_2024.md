# Research Findings

## Executive Summary

**Waterloo Region Healthcare Data Analysis (2020-2024)**
- Current Population (2024): **678,170** (Year-end estimate)
- Projected Population (2051): **923,000** 
- Regional Growth Rate: **1.5%** annually (15-year average)
- Healthcare capacity expansion is underway but facing significant demand pressures

---

## PILLAR 1: HEALTHCARE CAPACITY

### Hospital Infrastructure (2020-2024)

#### Major Hospital Facilities:
1. **Grand River Hospital** (Kitchener-Waterloo & Freeport campuses)
   - Part of newly formed Waterloo Regional Health Network (April 1, 2025)
   - Merging with St. Mary's General Hospital

2. **St. Mary's General Hospital** (Kitchener)
   - **197 beds** (adult acute-care)
   - Now part of Waterloo Regional Health Network

3. **Cambridge Memorial Hospital**
   - **52 new inpatient beds** added (expansion completed)
   - Total capacity after 2025 renovation: **108 beds** (59 medicine, 35 surgical, 14 rehabilitation)
   - Received **$11.2 million** Ontario funding for 22 additional beds
   - **$11.2 million** provincial investment (2024)

#### Provincial Context:
- Ontario has **lowest hospital bed rate per 1,000 population** compared to most provinces
- Ontario hospitals maintain **lowest cost per inpatient stay** in Canada
- **3,000 new hospital beds** announced provincially over 10 years (insufficient for aging demand)

#### New Hospital Development:
- **New Waterloo Regional Health Network Hospital**: 
  - **$15 million** total provincial investment ($10M + $5M)
  - Planned capacity: **Nearly 1,000 beds**
  - Site selection underway
  - Largest public infrastructure project in region for decades

#### Age Structure (2021 Census):
- **Median Age**: 38.0 years (younger than Ontario: 41.6 years)
- **Ages 0-14**: 17.3% (101,370 people)
- **Working Age 15-64**: 67.2% (higher than Ontario: 65.6%)
- **Ages 65+**: 15.5% (91,295 people) - **Growing rapidly**

#### Healthcare Demand Pressures:
- **Student Population**: 43,410 temporary students (included in population)
- **Households**: 242,880 (1.71% growth 2023-2024)
- **Aging Population**: Expected 2.04% annual growth in 65+ population

#### Emergency Department Utilization:
- Provincial ED wait times at **record levels**
- **21.78% Alternate Level of Care (ALC)** days in regional hospitals (2019-20)
- Record number of patients waiting in ED for admission

## PILLAR 3: HEALTHCARE ACCESS

### Wait Times Data (Ontario Health 2024-25):

#### Home Care Access:
- **50th percentile** community to first service: **7 days** (target: 7 days)
- **90th percentile** community to first service: **42 days** (target: 21 days) - **EXCEEDS TARGET**
- Hospital discharge to home care: **2 days median**, **14 days 90th percentile**

#### Long-term Care Wait Times:
- From community setting: **11-17 days** (2019-2023)
- From hospital: **7-8 days** (2019-2023)

#### Surgical Wait Times (Historical Waterloo Wellington LHIN):
- **Hip replacement**: 71.01% within target (target: 90%)
- **Knee replacement**: 69.20% within target (target: 90%)
- **Cataract surgery**: 71.00% within target (target: 90%)

### Family Doctor Access:
- Provincial physician shortage affecting region
- New **Ontario Health Teams** receiving **$2.25 million** each over 3 years
- **Cambridge North Dumfries OHT** established

## PILLAR 4: HEALTHCARE WORKFORCE

### Provincial Nursing Data (2024-2025):
- **Ontario Total Nurses**: 190,896 (3.88% year-over-year growth)
  - **RN**: 125,297 (65.6%)
  - **RPN**: 58,713 (30.8%)
  - **NP**: 5,367 (2.8%)

### Regional Workforce Challenges:
- Ontario added **more nurse practitioners in 2022** than any other province
- **Private NP services** charging $70-$90 per appointment (not OHIP covered)
- **Community commitment programs** for nurse retention
- **HealthForceOntario** initiatives active

### Physician Data:
- **College of Physicians and Surgeons Ontario (CPSO)** 2024 data shows continued growth
- **8,980 doctors** engaged through Quality Improvement Program
- New pathways to licensure for qualified doctors implemented

## PILLAR 5: HEALTHCARE OUTCOMES

### Life Expectancy:
- **Canada National (2023)**: 81.7 years (increased from 81.3 in 2022)
- **Ontario Context**: Following national trends
- **COVID-19 Impact**: 3-year decline ended in 2023

### Health System Performance:
- **Ontario health expenditure per capita**: **$4,889** (2022-23) - **Lowest in Canada**
- **Provincial program expenditure per capita**: **Lowest nationally**
- **Hospitalization rate**: Lowest in Canada
- **Average length of stay**: Lowest in Canada (tied with Quebec)

### Quality Indicators:
- **Preventable hospitalizations**: Data shows regional hospitals managing complex care
- **Repeat ED visits**: 16-25% for mental health and substance abuse conditions
- **30-day readmission rates**: Tracking within provincial standards

## FUTURE PROJECTIONS (2025-2050)

### Population Growth:
- **2024**: 678,170
- **2051 Projection**: **923,000** (Regional Official Plan)
- **Average Annual Growth**: 1.5%
- **Additional residents**: ~245,000 over 27 years

### Infrastructure Needs:
- **New hospital beds required**: Estimated 2.04% annually for aging population
- **New Waterloo Regional Health Network Hospital**: Planning for ~1,000 beds
- **Long-term care**: Declining from 60 to 56 beds per 1,000 residents 75+ (2024-2028)

### Healthcare Capacity Planning:
- **121,000 new homes** planned by 2051
- **15-minute neighbourhoods** concept for healthcare access
- **BestWR Vision 1 Million Scorecard**: Healthcare services "not advancing at pace needed"

### Provincial Investment Commitments:
- **$130.5 million** for Ontario Health Teams (3-year program)
- **$15 million** Community Stroke Rehabilitation expansion
- **Addictions Recovery Fund**: $8 million for mobile crisis response teams

---

## KEY CHALLENGES IDENTIFIED

1. **Capacity Gap**: Healthcare infrastructure not keeping pace with population growth
2. **Aging Demographics**: Rapid growth in 65+ population outpacing bed capacity
3. **Wait Times**: Exceeding targets for surgical procedures and home care
4. **Workforce**: Physician and nurse shortages despite growth
5. **Emergency Access**: Record ED wait times and ALC rates
