# Waterloo Region Healthcare Data Research Report
## Regional and Municipal Healthcare Analysis 2020-2024

**Research Date:** January 2025  
**Coverage Areas:** Region of Waterloo, Kitchener, Waterloo, Cambridge, North Dumfries, Wellesley, Wilmot, Woolwich  
**Time Period:** 2020-2024 with projections to 2050  

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This comprehensive research examines healthcare capacity, demand, access, workforce, and outcomes across Waterloo Region's 8 constituent areas. The region faces significant healthcare challenges driven by rapid population growth (1.5% annually) and an aging demographic, requiring substantial system expansion to meet future needs.

**Key Findings:**
- **Population Growth:** From 623,930 (2020) to 678,170 (2024), projected to reach 923,000 by 2051
- **Healthcare Gap:** 167 additional hospital beds needed immediately, with 1,030 additional staff required
- **Primary Care Crisis:** 64,900+ residents without family doctors as of 2022
- **Aging Population:** 15.5% aged 65+ (2021), requiring increased healthcare services

## PILLAR 1: HEALTHCARE CAPACITY

### Hospital Infrastructure

#### Major Hospitals (2024)
1. **Grand River Hospital** (Kitchener)
   - KW Campus and Freeport Campus
   - Merged with St. Mary's General Hospital (April 2025) to form Waterloo Regional Health Network (WRHN)

2. **St. Mary's General Hospital** (Kitchener)
   - Now part of WRHN

3. **Cambridge Memorial Hospital** (Cambridge)
   - Received $11.2M (2024) for 22 additional acute medical-surgical beds
   - Serves growing Cambridge community

#### Bed Capacity Analysis
- **Current Deficit:** 167 additional hospital beds needed (2023 CUPE report)
- **Required Growth:** 22% increase in bed capacity needed to meet demand over 4 years
- **New Hospital Project:** 400 additional acute care beds planned at University of Waterloo north campus

#### Healthcare Facilities Distribution
- **Primary Hospitals:** 3 major facilities serving 678,170 residents
- **Specialized Services:** Withdrawal management center (Grand River Hospital)
- **Future Capacity:** New WRHN hospital will increase system capacity by 500 beds

### Sources:
- CUPE Ontario Hospital Union Report (2023)
- Cambridge Memorial Hospital funding announcement (2024)
- Waterloo Regional Health Network development plans (2024-2025)

## PILLAR 2: HEALTHCARE DEMAND

### Population Demographics and Growth

#### Population Trends (2020-2024)
- **2020:** 623,930 residents
- **2021:** 614,000 (Statistics Canada Census)
- **2024:** 678,170 residents (year-end estimate)
- **Growth Rate:** 1.5% annually over past 15 years

#### Aging Population Demographics
- **Seniors (65+):** 91,295 persons (15.5% of total population, 2021)
- **Healthcare Utilization:** Seniors account for 59.5% of hospital bed days (2021/22)
- **Growth Rate:** 65+ age group growing at 3%+ annually, double the overall population rate

#### Future Population Projections
- **2051 Projection:** 923,000 residents (Provincial Growth Plan)
- **Vision 1 Million:** Potential to reach 1 million residents by 2050

### Healthcare Service Demand
- **Emergency Department Usage:** Higher proportion of ED visits from older adults
- **Aging-Related Demand:** Increasing need for complex care, longer hospital stays
- **Population Health Needs:** Growing demand for chronic disease management, specialized services

### Sources:
- Region of Waterloo Population Statistics (2024)
- Statistics Canada 2021 Census
- Provincial Growth Plan projections
- Financial Accountability Office health utilization data

## PILLAR 3: HEALTHCARE ACCESS

### Primary Care Access Crisis

#### Family Doctor Shortage (2022 Data)
- **Total Without Family Doctor:** 64,900+ residents in Kitchener, Waterloo, Cambridge areas
- **2020 Baseline:** 58,100 without family doctor
- **Growth in Gap:** 6,800 additional residents lost access (2020-2022)

#### Ontario Health Team Breakdown

##### KW4 Ontario Health Team
- **Coverage:** Kitchener, Waterloo, Wellesley, Wilmot, Woolwich
- **Population:** 387,572 residents
- **Without Family Doctor (2020):** 47,905
- **Without Family Doctor (2022):** 50,118
- **Increase:** 2,213 additional residents

##### Cambridge North Dumfries Ontario Health Team
- **Coverage:** Cambridge, North Dumfries
- **Population:** 146,241 residents
- **Without Family Doctor (2020):** 10,263
- **Without Family Doctor (2022):** 14,780
- **Increase:** 4,517 additional residents (44% increase)

### Healthcare Access Challenges
- **Physician Retirement:** 52% of family doctors considering retirement in next 5 years (Ontario-wide)
- **Medical Student Interest:** Only 42% considering family medicine, half wanting comprehensive care
- **Service Availability:** Limited after-hours and weekend primary care options

### Wait Times and Service Access
- **Home Care Wait Times:** 7-42 days from application to first service
- **Hospital Discharge to Home Care:** 2-14 days (50th-90th percentile)
- **Emergency Department Access:** Increased reliance on ED for primary care needs

### Sources:
- INSPIRE-PHC Research (2023)
- Ontario Family Physicians Association Report (2023)
- Ontario Health Team progress reports (2023-2024)

## PILLAR 4: HEALTHCARE WORKFORCE

### Healthcare Worker Shortage

#### Required Staffing Increases
- **Additional Staff Needed:** 1,030 healthcare workers required over 4 years (2023 estimate)
- **Growth Rate Required:** 22% increase in staffing levels to meet demand
- **Current Growth Rate:** Only 0.4% annually since 2020 (vs. 5.2% required annually)

#### Nursing Workforce
- **National Context:** Ontario added more nurse practitioners than any other province (2022)
- **Regional Challenges:** Higher vacancy rates in rural/remote areas affecting townships
- **Full-time Employment Growth:** +4.4% RNs, +2.8% RPNs, +7.2% NPs (provincial data)

#### Physician Workforce
- **Family Physicians:** Significant shortage across all 8 areas
- **Specialist Access:** Limited availability, increasing wait times
- **Rural Coverage:** Particular challenges in townships (Wellesley, Wilmot, Woolwich, North Dumfries)

### Healthcare Worker Distribution
- **Urban Concentration:** Most specialists in Kitchener, Waterloo, Cambridge
- **Rural Gaps:** Limited family physician and specialist coverage in townships
- **Service Integration:** Ontario Health Teams working to coordinate care delivery

### Sources:
- Canadian Institute for Health Information (CIHI) reports (2024)
- College of Nurses of Ontario statistics (2024)
- CUPE hospital workforce analysis (2023)

- **Regional Context:** Waterloo Region population younger than Ontario/Canada average
- **Health Outcomes:** Generally positive due to higher income levels and education
- **Aging Impact:** Growing seniors population requiring specialized care

#### Healthcare System Performance
- **Emergency Department Usage:** Increasing reliance for primary care
- **Preventable Hospitalizations:** Data indicates system strain from lack of primary care
- **Chronic Disease Management:** Challenges with coordination and access

- **Education:** High education levels supporting health literacy
- **Social Determinants:** Strong community infrastructure supporting health outcomes

### Health Services Outcomes

- **Community Health:** Strong public health infrastructure and programming
- **Mental Health Services:** CMHA Waterloo Wellington providing community support

## HEALTHCARE PROJECTIONS 2025-2050

### Infrastructure Development

#### New Hospital Project (WRHN @ University)
- **Timeline:** Planning phase advanced with $15.8M government investment (2024)
- **Capacity:** 400 additional acute care beds
- **Design:** HDR and KPMB Architects awarded design contract
- **Impact:** Will serve growing region and beyond

#### System Expansion Requirements
- **Hospital Beds:** Minimum 167 additional beds needed immediately
- **Staffing:** 1,030 additional healthcare workers required
- **Growth Rate:** 22% capacity increase needed across system

### Population and Demand Projections

#### Demographics (2025-2051)
- **Total Population:** 923,000 by 2051 (48% increase from 2024)
- **Seniors Growth:** 65+ population expected to double by 2050
- **Healthcare Demand:** Exponential increase in complex care needs

#### Service Requirements
- **Primary Care:** Significant expansion of family medicine practices needed
- **Specialized Services:** Increased capacity for chronic disease management, surgery, diagnostics
- **Community Care:** Enhanced home care and community support services

### Regional Planning Initiatives

#### Vision 1 Million Scorecard (2025)
- **Objective:** Assess readiness for 1 million residents by 2050
- **Healthcare Assessment:** Current healthcare services "not advancing at pace needed"
- **Gap Areas:** Hospital capacity, primary care access, specialized services

#### Strategic Investments Required
- **Infrastructure:** New hospitals, expanded existing facilities
- **Workforce:** Massive recruitment and training initiatives
- **Technology:** Enhanced digital health services and telemedicine
- **Community Services:** Expanded public health and prevention programs
