# Comprehensive Healthcare Data Sources for Waterloo Region (2020-2024)
## Covering All 8 Municipalities: Region of Waterloo, Kitchener, Waterloo, Cambridge, North Dumfries, Wellesley, Wilmot, Woolwich

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This comprehensive research document identifies healthcare data sources across the five pillars of healthcare for Waterloo Region and its eight constituent municipalities. The analysis covers capacity, demand, access, workforce, and outcomes data from university research, health networks, hospital reports, and credible government sources for the period 2020-2024, with projections to 2050.

**Key Population Context:**
- Current Population (2024): ~650,000-675,000
- Projected Population (2050): 923,000-1,000,000 
- Median Age (2021): 38.0 years (younger than Ontario: 41.6 years)
- Seniors (65+): 15.5% (2021), projected to increase 33% by 2050

### Hospital Infrastructure & Bed Capacity

#### Major Healthcare Facilities:
1. **Waterloo Regional Health Network (WRHN)** - Formed April 2025
   - Merger of Grand River Hospital and St. Mary's General Hospital
   - Named one of Top 100 hospitals in Canada (Newsweek 2026)
   - Planning new 500-bed hospital at University of Waterloo

2. **Grand River Hospital Corporation (Pre-merger)**
   - Kitchener-Waterloo Health Centre
   - Freeport Health Centre
   - Financial Data (2024): $25.6M cash, $34.7M capital investments

3. **St. Mary's General Hospital (Pre-merger)**
   - Financial Data (2024): $29.4M cash, $14.1M capital investments
   - Strategic Plan 2021-2026 focusing on capacity expansion

#### Capacity Expansion Projects:
- **New WRHN Hospital**: 500-bed expansion planned
- **Provincial Investment**: $15 million total ($5M initial + $10M additional)

## CONCLUSION

Waterloo Region faces significant healthcare system challenges as it grows toward 1 million residents by 2050. While the region has strong healthcare foundations with the newly merged WRHN and university research partnerships, substantial capacity expansion and workforce development will be required to meet projected demand. The region's younger demographic profile provides advantages, but the anticipated growth in seniors and chronic disease burden will require proactive planning and investment across all five healthcare pillars.
