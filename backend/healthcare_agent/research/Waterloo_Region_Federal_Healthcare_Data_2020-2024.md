# Federal Healthcare Data for Waterloo Region (2020-2024)
## Comprehensive Analysis of Healthcare System Capacity, Demand, Access, Workforce, and Outcomes

**Research Date:** December 2024  
**Geographic Coverage:** Region of Waterloo and 8 constituent areas  
**Data Sources:** Statistics Canada, CIHI, CMHC, Infrastructure Canada  

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This comprehensive analysis examines federal healthcare data for Waterloo Region from 2020-2024, covering all 8 areas: Region of Waterloo, Kitchener, Waterloo, Cambridge, North Dumfries, Wellesley, Wilmot, and Woolwich. The region faces significant healthcare challenges including capacity constraints, growing demand from population growth, access barriers, and workforce shortages.

### Key Findings:
- **Population Growth:** Region grew to 678,170 by end-2024 (15-year average 1.5% annually)
- **Hospital Capacity:** Ontario has 2.3 hospital beds per 1,000 population (below national 2.5 average)
- **Workforce Shortages:** National projections show 78,000 doctor shortage by 2031, 117,600 nurse shortage by 2030
- **Wait Times:** Ontario median wait time increased from 14.1 weeks (2014) to 23.6 weeks (2024)
- **Major Investment:** Ontario investing $15M for new Waterloo Regional Health Network hospital (opening 2034)

## PILLAR 1: HEALTHCARE CAPACITY

### Hospital Beds and Facilities

#### National/Provincial Context (2020-2024)
- **Canada Hospital Beds (2023):** 2.49 per 1,000 people (decreased from 2.53 in 2022)
- **Ontario Hospital Beds:** 2.3 per 1,000 population
- **OECD Average:** 4.6 beds per 1,000 population
- **Ranking:** Ontario ranks among lowest in OECD countries

**Source:** Trading Economics, OECD Health Statistics, Ontario Health Coalition

#### Regional Infrastructure
- **Hospital Expenditure:** Ontario has lowest hospital expenditure per capita among provinces ($1,885 per capita, 2023)
- **Efficiency Metrics:** Ontario maintains lowest hospitalization rate in Canada and shortest average length of stay

#### New Infrastructure Investment
**New Waterloo Regional Health Network Hospital:**
- **Total Investment:** $15M planning funding (as of July 2025)
  - $10M additional funding (July 2025)  
  - $5M previous investment
- **Location:** University of Waterloo lands, north Waterloo
- **Expected Opening:** 2034
- **Capacity:** New acute-care hospital with expanded emergency services

**Source:** Ontario Ministry of Health, Infrastructure Canada

### Healthcare Facilities Database
- **Open Database of Healthcare Facilities (ODHF):** Statistics Canada maintains comprehensive listing of health facilities across Canada, including Waterloo Region
- **Data Availability:** Harmonized listing available as open data (CSV format)

## PILLAR 3: HEALTHCARE ACCESS

### Wait Times (2020-2024)

#### Ontario Specific Data
- **2014 Median Wait Time:** 14.1 weeks
- **2024 Median Wait Time:** 23.6 weeks (67% increase)
- **2025 Improvement:** 19.2 weeks (shortest among provinces)
- **National Average (2024):** 30.0 weeks GP referral to treatment

**Provincial Comparison (2025):**
- Ontario: 19.2 weeks (best)
- British Columbia: 32.2 weeks  
- Quebec: 32.5 weeks
- New Brunswick: 60.9 weeks (worst)

**Source:** Fraser Institute, Canadian Institute for Health Information

### Family Doctor Access
- **National Crisis:** Half of Canadians either don't have family doctor or struggle to access
- **Regional Impact:** Atlantic Canada and Quebec report worst access (21-31% no family doctor)
- **Access Difficulties:** 29-31% report difficult access to existing family doctor

### Emergency Department Pressures
- **Record Wait Times:** Hospitals face record-setting emergency department wait times
- **Admission Backlogs:** Record numbers waiting in emergency for hospital admission
- **Alternate Level of Care (ALC):** Patients waiting in hospital for appropriate community services

## PILLAR 4: HEALTHCARE WORKFORCE

### National Workforce Context (2024)

#### Physicians
- **Total Doctors (2024):** 99,555 in Canada
- **Rate:** 241 doctors per 100,000 population
- **Projected Shortage:** 78,000 doctors by 2031

#### Nursing Workforce
- **Registered Nurses (2023):** 477,980 eligible to practice
- **Nurse Practitioners (2024):** 9,891 (9.5% increase from 2023)
- **National Rate:** 11.04 nurses per 1,000 people (2023)
- **Projected Shortage:** 117,600 nurses by 2030

**Source:** Canadian Institute for Health Information (CIHI), Health Canada

### Workforce Distribution Challenges
- **Regional Variations:** Significant disparities in healthcare worker distribution
- **Rural Access:** Particular challenges in rural townships (North Dumfries, Wellesley, Wilmot, Woolwich)
