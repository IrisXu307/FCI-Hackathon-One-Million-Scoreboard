# WATERLOO REGION EMPLOYMENT DATA - PILLAR 2: WORKFORCE

## Executive Summary
Waterloo Region consistently maintains higher labour force participation rates than provincial and national averages, though unemployment rates have been experiencing some fluctuation.

## Key Data Points

### Labour Force Participation Rate
- **2020**: 68.7% (KCW CMA: 338,600 in labour force / 492,900 population 15+)
- **2021**: 68.9% (Waterloo Region)
- **2022**: 69.5% (Waterloo Region)
  - **Comparison**: Higher than Ontario (65.4%) and Canada (65.4%)
- **2024**: Data shows continued strength in participation

### Unemployment Rate Trends
- **2020**: Calculated at ~9.6% (KCW CMA: 32,400 unemployed / 338,600 labour force)
- **2021**: 6.3% (Waterloo Region)
- **2022**: 5.7% (Waterloo Region)
  - **Comparison**: Slightly higher than Ontario (5.6%) but lower than Canada (5.3%)
- **2024 June**: 6.5% (Kitchener-Waterloo-Cambridge CMA)
- **2026 February**: 8.6% (recent data showing increase)

### Labour Force Composition (2020 Baseline)
- **Total Population 15+**: 492,900
- **Labour Force**: 338,600 people
- **Employed**: 306,200 people  
- **Unemployed**: 32,400 people
- **Participation Rate**: 68.7%

### Geographic Coverage Notes
KCW CMA includes:
- Cities: Kitchener, Cambridge, Waterloo
- Townships: Woolwich, North Dumfries, Wilmot

### Employment Recovery by Sector (2020 Detailed Monthly Data)
**Goods-Producing Sector**: 95% recovery rate
- Agriculture: 80% recovery
- Construction: 90% recovery  
- Manufacturing: 99% recovery

**Services-Producing Sector**: 97% recovery rate
- Professional, Scientific & Technical Services: 117% (growth)
- Health Care & Social Assistance: 128% (strong growth)
- Other Services: 108% (growth)
- Transportation & Warehousing: 122% (strong growth)
- Educational Services: 79% (below pre-pandemic levels)
- Accommodation & Food Services: 52% (most impacted)
- Information, Culture & Recreation: 71% (below pre-pandemic)

## Comparative Performance
- **Regional Strength**: Consistently higher participation rates than provincial/national averages
- **Recent Trends**: Some softening in unemployment rates from 2022 lows
- **Sectoral Resilience**: Most sectors recovered to pre-pandemic levels by end of 2020
